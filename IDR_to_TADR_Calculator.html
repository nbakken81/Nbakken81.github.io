3 · Workload & geometry

WL = Wconv + CI × WIMRT. Or divide total weekly MU by your survey MU rate and enter the clock directly below.

4 · Busiest hour (uncontrolled only)

Helper: fractions in the hour × Gy per fraction × Uh. Bound Uh from the real peak-hour plans (0.5 is a common bound) — do not reuse the long-run 0.25. For leakage or scatter points use the full hour's delivery with U = 1.

5 · Scatter estimate (design check — optional)

A measured reading already contains the scatter fraction. Use this only to predict a scatter IDR when there is nothing to measure yet.

What the symbols mean

IDRmeter reading with the machine at maximum output
Ḋ₀machine output at isocenter during that reading (Gy/h)
Wtreatment workload — dose delivered at isocenter each week (Gy/wk)
WLleakage workload — W inflated by the IMRT factor, because leakage tracks MU
Uuse factor — fraction of beam time aimed at this barrier (1 for leakage and scatter)
Toccupancy factor — fraction of the week the most-exposed person is there
ascatter fraction — scattered dose at 1 m per unit dose at the patient, per 400 cm²
tthe clock — hours of beam per week relevant to this point

Units: W and Ḋ₀ are both in gray, so the gray cancels and the clock is pure time — the meter's unit carries straight through to the answer. 1 mR ≈ 1 mrem ≈ 10 µSv is the field convention (strictly 1 mR = 8.76 µGy air kerma, so the convention runs ~14% conservative).

Method follows NCRP Report No. 151 in simplified notation; dose limits per 10 CFR 20.1201 and 20.1301 as incorporated in Pennsylvania through 25 Pa.
